# Phase 2 - Business lens

[Dangote Cement Plc](https://cement.dangote.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/Dangote-Cememt-Plc-2025-Annual-Report.pdf) is Africa's largest cement manufacturer, boasting a total production capacity of 55 million tonnes per annum (Mta) across Sub-Saharan Africa. As the dominant industrial entity listed on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), the company commands a ~60% market share in its home country while aggressively scaling its Pan-African footprint. [1, 2, 3]

---

## 1. What does Dangote Cement sell? (Product)

Dangote Cement primarily produces and markets high-grade Ordinary Portland Cement (OPC) packaged in standard 50kg bags and sold in bulk for heavy construction. The company offers tailored grades (such as 32.5, 42.5, and 52.5 strength grades) optimized for different structural requirements. Additionally, it sells and exports bulk clinker—the core intermediate nodular material used to grind into finished cement. [4, 5, 6, 7]

## 2. Who buys it? (Customers)

The customer base is multi-layered and segmented into three main groups: [5]

- Individual Retail Buyers & Artisans: Everyday consumers, local bricklayers, and private individuals executing residential self-build projects. [5, 8]
- Corporate Real Estate & Infrastructure Developers: Commercial construction firms handling high-rise buildings, private housing estates, and industrial complexes. [5]
- Public Sector / Government Contractors: Entities executing large-scale public infrastructure projects, such as highways, bridges, dams, and ports. [8]

## 3. How does the company make money? (Revenue model)

The revenue model is structured around a high-volume, cost-leadership production and distribution framework: [3]

- Direct Sales: Volume-driven sales of bagged and bulk cement through an extensive network of distributors, wholesalers, and corporate clients. [5, 9]
- Clinker & Cement Exports: Cross-border maritime and land-based exports of clinker and finished cement from hub countries like Nigeria to regional markets like Ghana, Cameroon, and Côte d'Ivoire. [1, 7]
- Foreign Exchange Neutralization: Exporting helps generate raw foreign exchange revenues, cushioning the company against local currency devaluations. [1]

## 4. What are the biggest cost drivers?

Operating as a capital-intensive manufacturing business, its primary cost drivers include: [10]

- Energy and Fuel Mix: Powering heavy kilns requires a massive energy mix comprising natural gas, imported or local coal, and alternative biomass fuels. [1, 10]
- Logistics and Distribution: Moving heavy bulk material across vast territories requires massive fleets. Dangote mitigates this through thousands of self-owned delivery trucks, recently transitioning heavily to Compressed Natural Gas (CNG) to optimize fuel costs. [8, 11]
- Raw Materials: Sourcing limestone and clay (primarily from self-owned quarries via vertical integration), alongside importing additives like gypsum. [3, 9]

## 5. Where does it operate? (Geography — Nigeria vs. Pan-African)

Dangote Cement employs a diversified geographic model split across two main segments: [11]

- Nigeria Hub: The core cash engine. It operates major integrated plants in Obajana (the largest in Sub-Saharan Africa), Ibese, and Gboko, accounting for 35.3Mta of its total capacity.
- Pan-African Operations: Operations span 10 additional countries across West, Central, and Southern Africa. This includes integrated manufacturing facilities and grinding terminals in countries such as South Africa, Ethiopia, Tanzania, Zambia, Senegal, Cameroon, Republic of Congo, Ghana, Sierra Leone, and a plant commissioned in Côte d'Ivoire. [1, 6, 12, 13]

## BUA Cement Plc

- What they do: BUA Cement Plc manufactures and markets high-quality Ordinary Portland Cement grades, leveraging highly automated, ultra-modern integrated plants to drive a high-volume production model.
- Where they operate: The company operates predominantly in Nigeria, running massive manufacturing hubs in Sokoto State (Northern region) and Edo State (Southern region), which allow it to efficiently supply both domestic markets and land-border export routes.
- Why they are comparable: BUA Cement is Dangote's fiercest and closest domestic rival on the Nigerian Exchange (NGX), forming the second-largest piece of Nigeria's cement oligopoly with an aggressive capacity expansion strategy that directly challenges Dangote’s market share.

---

## Lafarge Africa Plc

- What they do: Lafarge Africa Plc produces and distributes a diverse portfolio of construction materials, including standard bagged cement, ready-mix concrete, and specialized chemical solutions for complex infrastructure projects.
- Where they operate: Headquartered and publicly listed in Nigeria, the company maintains strategically located manufacturing footprints across Ewekoro and Sagamu (South-West), Mfamosing (South-South), and Ashaka (North-East).
- Why they are comparable: As the third major player listed on the NGX, it serves as an ideal operational benchmark; its backing by global building materials giant Holcim Group allows for a direct comparison of multinational corporate governance and efficiency against Dangote's localized model.

---

## PPC Ltd (Pretoria Portland Cement)

- What they do: PPC Ltd is a leading industrial producer of cement, aggregates, metallurgical-grade lime, burnt dolomite, and ready-mix concrete materials.
- Where they operate: Headquartered and listed in South Africa on the Johannesburg Stock Exchange (JSE), its footprint extends into several Sub-Saharan nations, including Zimbabwe, Botswana, and Rwanda.
- Why they are comparable: PPC provides a vital pan-African comparative framework because it competes head-to-head with Dangote’s South African subsidiary (Sephaku Cement) and offers a direct look at cross-border logistics, regional economic pressures, and pricing outside of Nigeria.

## Part C — The Investing Use Case

### Who uses this analysis?
An equity analyst or portfolio manager at an Africa-focused fund, or an 
individual investor with a long-term (5-10 year) horizon, considering whether 
to allocate capital to DANGCEM. The peer set (BUA, Lafarge, PPC) represents 
the realistic alternatives within the same sector and region.

### What decision would they make with it?
The decision is allocation, not timing. Given a fixed amount of capital and 
four cement producers to choose from, which one offers the best value 
relative to its earnings, assets, and growth? The relative valuation 
snapshot informs this decision by answering: "Is Dangote's price cheap, fair, 
or expensive compared to what you'd pay for the same fundamentals elsewhere?"

### What would a wrong answer cost them?
1. Direct loss: If the analysis says "cheap" and the investor buys, but 
   Dangote was actually priced fairly (or expensively) relative to peers, 
   the investor overpaid.
2. Opportunity cost: The capital could have been allocated to a cheaper 
   peer with stronger fundamentals, producing higher returns.
3. In a portfolio context: If DANGCEM is over-weighted based on a flawed 
   "cheap" conclusion, the portfolio's sector exposure becomes unbalanced, 
   creating concentration risk.

The cost is not just the specific position—it is the portfolio-level 
misallocation of capital that follows from the wrong signal.
